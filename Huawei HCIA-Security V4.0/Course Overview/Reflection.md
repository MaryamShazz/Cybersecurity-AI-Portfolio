# Reflection

The most valuable part of this course for me was probably the NAT and hot standby modules. I'd worked with firewalls before in a surface-level way, but going through how source NAT, a NAT server, and VRRP/HRP-based hot standby fit together made the "how does an enterprise network actually stay up and translate traffic correctly" picture click in a way I hadn't gotten from reading about it on my own.

What challenged me most was the cryptography and PKI section — symmetric and asymmetric encryption made sense on their own, but connecting that to how a certificate chain and a CA actually establish trust took a couple of passes through the material before it stuck. I also paused the course for a while, around 80% through, to focus on some of my own projects, and coming back to it later meant re-grounding myself in a few of the earlier modules before finishing the rest.

This course fits directly into what I'm trying to build toward. I'm working on projects like a SOC dashboard and a network anomaly detector, and this gave me the underlying network security and firewall context those projects sit on top of. It's also directly relevant to the cybersecurity and digital forensics master's programs I'm applying to, since a lot of what's taught here (firewall policy, VPNs, intrusion prevention) is exactly the kind of practical network defense knowledge I want to keep building on.
